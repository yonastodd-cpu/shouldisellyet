What this is

ShouldISellYet.com answers one question for homeowners: is the market where my home sits turning against me? Enter a ZIP code and you get a plain-English verdict — HOLD, WATCH, or ACT — computed from public housing-market data, free, with no account. Paid products add the homeowner's own numbers (equity, walk-away proceeds, lock-in cost) and monthly alerts when their market shifts. It is operated by Yayday LLC and is not a brokerage; it does not list, sell, or value individual homes.

How the verdict is computed

Four public signals, each with a danger line drawn from how markets behaved before past national downturns. The thresholds are published, not proprietary — the point is that anyone can check our work.

You can check it on specific markets: the methodology page carries recomputed case studies — every date and lead time re-derived from the source data under today's published thresholds, including one market that crossed a danger line and then recovered. We publish the miss alongside the hits on purpose.

HOLD — no lines crossed WATCH — early signals moving ACT — multiple lines crossed

The same signals are read in the opposite direction too: a market with zero warnings and at least three strength signals reads as a strong seller's market. Every dial on the site discloses its source counts, its formula in words, and why its line sits where it does.

The verdict often says don't sell — by design

A tool built to generate real-estate leads would find urgency everywhere. Ours doesn't. Across 24,619 scored ZIP codes in the latest data, the verdict is a flat HOLD or a strong seller's market for two-thirds of the country. Telling most people to do nothing is the product working correctly.

We also test the thresholds rather than assert them. Replaying every year-end signal snapshot the source data supports — 182,644 ZIP-years, 2012–2026 — against the federal government's own annual house-price index for the following year, the ladder holds: markets reading HOLD saw prices fall the next year 11.3% of the time, WATCH 18.9%, ACT 28.1%.

Where the data comes from

Verdicts are computed from public housing-market data. Data provided by Redfin, a national real estate brokerage. An independent cross-check uses Realtor.com's residential listings database; mortgage rates come from Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey; the threshold backtest and an official price benchmark use the Federal Housing Finance Agency's ZIP-level index; place names come from GeoNames. No source sponsors, endorses, or partners with this site, and no source has any influence over a verdict.

Data refreshes automatically when new figures publish, and every page shows the month its data runs through.

Free to cite: ShouldISellYet Research

Every month we publish the Warning-Sign Index — the share of scored U.S. ZIP markets showing warning signs, computed from the same danger lines described above and charted back to 2012. Each release carries national, state and metro numbers, the ZIPs that crossed the danger line that month, and a one-paragraph local angle for every state.

The data is downloadable and free to use with citation ("Source: ShouldISellYet Research") — state, metro and ZIP-level CSVs, plus the full index history. Charts are published at 1200×675 with attribution already baked in, so they can run as-is. Definitions, the FHFA backtest, and a versioned changelog live on the methodology page; the index definition is fixed, and any change to it is versioned and restated rather than applied silently.
